# Interview Prep Guide

Rules the agent uses to prepare the candidate for an interview for a specific role.

## What the prep section should contain
1. **Company talking points (3–5):** facts from the company research and recent news the candidate should know and can reference: business model, recent moves, strategic priorities, challenges.
2. **Likely questions (6–8):** a mix of
   - motivation: "Why this company?", "Why this role?", "Why now?"
   - experience: questions based on the posting's must-have requirements
   - behavioural: "Tell me about a time when..." tied to the role's core challenges
   - one or two role-specific case or problem-solving questions, if the role is strategy, consulting or operations
3. **Stories to use (3–4):** for each, which achievement from the achievement bank answers which likely question, in STAR form (Situation, Task, Action, Result).
4. **Gaps to prepare for:** must-haves the candidate lacks and an honest way to address each one.
5. **Questions to ask the interviewer (3–5):** specific to the company and role, showing preparation. Avoid questions answered on the company website.

## STAR method
- Situation: the context in one or two sentences.
- Task: what the candidate was responsible for.
- Action: what the candidate personally did (use "I", not "we").
- Result: the outcome, with a number if available, and what was learned.
- Aim for answers of about 2 minutes.

## Common question types by role
- **Strategy / consulting / corporate development:** structuring an ambiguous problem, market sizing, prioritization, working with senior stakeholders.
- **Operations:** scaling, process design, KPIs and performance management, handling incidents.
- **Chief of Staff / programme lead:** managing multiple workstreams, influencing without authority, stakeholder alignment.
- **AI / automation roles:** identifying use cases, measuring ROI, change management, risks and compliance.

## Rules
- Only build stories from the achievement bank. Do not invent situations or numbers.
- Keep everything specific to the company and role; avoid generic interview advice.

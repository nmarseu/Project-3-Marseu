# Cover Letter Guidelines

Rules the agent uses to write a tailored cover letter.

## Hard rules
- Only use achievements, numbers and experiences found in the candidate's achievement bank. If something the job asks for is not there, do not claim it.
- Never copy CV bullets word for word. The letter explains why the experience matters for this company and role.
- Length: 250–350 words, 3–4 short paragraphs. No bullet lists.
- Address a named person if one is known; otherwise use "Dear Hiring Team".

## Structure
1. **Opening (2–3 sentences):** the role, and a specific, genuine reason for interest in this company, based on the company research (a recent move, a product, the market they are in). No generic openings like "I am writing to apply for...".
2. **Proof (1–2 paragraphs):** two or three achievements from the achievement bank that map directly to the role's top requirements. Each one: what was done, the result, and why it is relevant here.
3. **Fit and value:** what the candidate would bring in the first months, connected to the company's current situation or challenges.
4. **Close (1–2 sentences):** a confident, simple close and a call to talk. No excessive gratitude.

## Tone
- Confident, direct and specific. Short sentences.
- Match the company's tone: more formal for corporates and consulting, more direct for startups.
- Avoid clichés: "passionate", "dynamic", "team player", "I believe I would be a great fit", "synergy".
- Avoid AI-sounding phrasing and filler. Every sentence should carry information.

## Handling gaps
- If a must-have is missing, the letter can briefly bridge it with the closest real experience (e.g. adjacent industry, transferable skill), without pretending.
- Career changes should be framed as a deliberate move with a clear thread connecting past roles to the target role.

# Future GTM sprints

The MVP works end to end for one candidate and one job at a time, sent through a form, delivered by email. The three sprints below are the next steps towards paying customers. None of this work is done yet.

**Positioning:** career services teams prepare candidates faster, without the risk of AI-invented experience. The agent does the research and first drafts; coaches spend their time on feedback.

**Key assumption to test:** coaches spend 1–2 hours preparing each application by hand. Sprint 1 measures this.

---

## Sprint 1: Design-partner pilot

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Prove that the agent saves coaches real time and that its reports are good enough to use with light edits. |
| **Target buyer / user** | Head of career services at one or two coding bootcamps in Berlin (buyer); their career coaches (users). |
| **Channel / motion** | Founder-led outreach through the bootcamp network; a free 4-week pilot in exchange for weekly feedback and data on time spent. |
| **Key deliverable** | Pilot package: a coach version of the form, a one-page onboarding guide, a feedback form attached to every report, and a time log comparing manual and agent-assisted prep. |
| **Success metric** | Prep time per application drops by at least 50% against the coaches' own baseline; at least 70% of reports rated "usable with light edits"; one letter of intent to pay after the pilot. |

---

## Sprint 2: From pilot to paid

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Turn the pilot into a sellable offer and sign the first paying customers. |
| **Target buyer** | Bootcamps and university career centres in the DACH region with 100+ job-seeking graduates a year. |
| **Channel / motion** | Case-study-led outbound: the Sprint 1 results as a one-page case study, sent to about 30 career services leads, followed by a 20-minute live demo. |
| **Key deliverable** | A pricing model (per report, or a monthly plan per coach); a GDPR pack (data processing agreement, no-storage policy, EU hosting); delivery from a branded sender domain instead of Gmail. |
| **Success metric** | Three paying customers by the end of the sprint; at least 30% of demos converted to a paid trial. |

---

## Sprint 3: Outplacement channel partner

| | |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | Reach more volume through one partner instead of selling to institutions one by one. |
| **Target buyer** | Outplacement firms, which companies pay to help laid-off employees find new jobs. Each firm supports many candidates at once. |
| **Channel / motion** | Partnership: a white-label version under the partner's brand, sold with a revenue share or volume pricing. |
| **Key deliverable** | White-label report and form (partner logo, colours, sender address); saved candidate profiles so coaches don't re-enter achievements; a partner dashboard showing reports generated and fit scores. |
| **Success metric** | One partner agreement signed; 200+ reports a month generated through the partner within 60 days of launch. |

---

## What stays out of scope

- Paid advertising and consumer (B2C) marketing: the cost of reaching individual job seekers is high, and coaches are a more efficient channel.
- Applying to jobs on the candidate's behalf: the product prepares people, it doesn't automate applications.
